# Office Holder Terms inherit party from the preceding Term instead of leaving party Membership unknown

**Status:** Accepted

When a Term's `party` field names a chamber Office Holder role (Speaker, Deputy-Speaker, President, Deputy-President), OpenAustralia has overwritten the real party in that field rather than reporting it alongside the office. The first implementation left party Membership blank for the duration — a real, visible gap in a politician's party history every time they held a chamber office, even though they didn't actually leave their party. We decided Interpretation should instead carry the party forward from the most recent preceding Term with a real (non-Office-Holder) party, and keep the corresponding Party Branch Membership open through the Office Holder period. This is a deliberate inference on top of what OpenAustralia literally states, not a straight read of the source data — chosen because leaving a known-wrong gap is worse than a reasonable inference, but it does mean interpretation is trusting continuity over the raw API value.

**Known limitation, accepted:** this inference is factually wrong for the two Speakers who followed the British tradition of resigning their party on election — Frederick Holder (1901, the first-ever Speaker) and Peter Slipper (2011) — both of whom were genuinely independent while serving. OpenAustralia's data can't distinguish these from ordinary Speakers who kept party membership, so there's no data-driven way to do better. Holder is excluded entirely by the 50-year ingestion window (see ADR 0004). Slipper is a known, publicised, one-off case — corrected manually via admin tooling after ingestion rather than special-cased in Interpretation.
